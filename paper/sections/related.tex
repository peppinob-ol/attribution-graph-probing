\section{Related Work: Attribution Graphs, Autointerp, and Causal Validation}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Attribution graphs and replacement models.}
Attribution graphs operationalize feature$\to$logit pathways in a local replacement model that linearizes the residual stream through CLT or SAE features and freezes attention~\cite{ameisen2025circuittracing,lindsey2025biology}. The Neuronpedia platform exposes these graphs and per-feature cards at scale~\cite{neuronpedia2025,lindsey2025landscape}, and \texttt{circuit-tracer} provides the open-source reference implementation~\cite{circuittracer2025}. Our work operates entirely \emph{downstream} of attribution: we do not propose a new attribution method, but we do produce supernodes that the same Neuronpedia tooling can render and pin.

\paragraph{Sparse features and dictionary learning.}
Sparse Autoencoders and Cross-Layer Transcoders expose sparse, often monosemantic features that make replacement-model analyses tractable~\cite{bricken2023monosemanticity,templeton2024scaling,crosscoders2024,marks2024dictionary}. We treat the CLT-HP dictionary as fixed and study how to \emph{group} its features into circuit-level units.

\paragraph{Feature labeling and autointerp.}
Automated interpretation of individual features by LLM scoring~\cite{bills2023autointerp,templeton2024scaling} produces per-feature labels from corpus activations but does not yield circuit-level groupings calibrated to a target logit, and is not validated by intervention. We take the complementary route: prompt-conditioned, circuit-level grouping with explicit causal validation.

\paragraph{Concurrent work on causal circuits with transcoders.}
\citet{hannaameisen2026planning} use cross-layer transcoder feature circuits to study whether language models latently plan rhyming words, structuring their analysis around two falsifiability conditions (predict-token-before-emission, causally-affect-token). Our two-condition framing of \cref{def:opuseful} (Specificity, Matched-Control) is directly inspired by their template, applied to the more general question of supernode label utility. \citet{marks2024dictionary} apply sparse-feature circuit analysis to subject--verb agreement; we contribute a domain-general harness with structurally matched random controls.

\paragraph{Steering with sparse features.}
Single-feature steering with high multipliers (Anthropic's $M{\sim}20$ paradigm) has been used to demonstrate concept-specific behavior~\cite{templeton2024scaling,kharlapenko2024selfsteer}. We generalize to multi-feature concept-aligned swaps and find empirically that the canonical $M{=}20$ default is over-steering in our setting (\cref{sec:results}), with the optimal $M$ bimodal at $\sim$2.4 and $\sim$6.9.

\paragraph{Causal interpretability and matched random controls.}
\citet{geiger2025causal} formalize three levels of interpretive claim and emphasize that \emph{distinguishing} levels requires intervention. \citet{heap2025sparse} show that randomly initialized transformers can score competitively on common interpretability metrics, undermining metrics that lack causal anchoring. Our matched-random-control protocol (\cref{def:matched}) is designed to address this critique directly: any structural property a random feature set shares with the labeled one (count, layer histogram, eligibility) is held constant; only the concept alignment changes.

\paragraph{Why we do not include a geometric-clustering baseline.}
A grouping derived from a behavior-grounded signature outperforms a purely geometric grouping (cosine, layer-adjacency, k-means, HDBSCAN) on behavior-grounded metrics by construction, so the comparison is structurally biased in our favor; and there is no canonical ``feature clustering'' baseline in the SAE/CLT literature to anchor the comparison. We instead validate via intervention against matched random controls (\cref{def:matched}), which is robust to the bias and \emph{applicable to any grouping method}---including future learned ones. We discuss this choice as a limitation in \cref{sec:limitations}.
