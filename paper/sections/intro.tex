\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Attribution graphs operationalize the feature-to-logit pathways in a language model but do not scale to human interpretation. A single Gemma-2-2B-it graph for a short factual prompt routinely contains 600--5{,}000 cross-layer transcoder (CLT) features and edges between them~\cite{ameisen2025circuittracing,lindsey2025biology}, and manual reading by an experienced circuit tracer has been reported to take on the order of two hours per prompt~\cite{neuronpedia2025podcast}. As mechanistic interpretability moves from individual case studies to large-scale catalogs of circuits~\cite{hannaameisen2026planning,marks2024dictionary}, automated circuit-level grouping tied to behavior is required.

Existing automated feature-interpretation pipelines~\cite{bills2023autointerp,paulo2024autointerp,templeton2024scaling} label individual features from corpus activations. The labels are per-feature, do not produce circuit-level groupings calibrated to a particular prompt's target logit, and are not themselves validated by causal intervention on the model. What is missing is a prompt-conditioned grouping of features into circuit-level units together with a procedure that tests whether the resulting labels support the causal story they suggest.

We propose \emph{probe prompting}, a transparent rule-based pipeline that converts an attribution graph into a compact set of concept-aligned supernodes. For each candidate feature in the graph we run a small set of concept-targeted probe prompts and summarize the feature's responses as a Cross-Prompt Activation Signature (CPAS). Deterministic threshold rules map each signature to one of four functional roles---Semantic-Dictionary, Semantic-Concept, Relationship, Say-X---and features that share role and name are merged into a supernode. Every assignment is traceable to a specific threshold crossing, so a researcher can audit and edit the grouping.

The paper's central evaluand is an \emph{operationally useful} supernode label. A label for entity $e$ is operationally useful when (i)~intervening on the labeled supernode redirects the model's output toward $e$ (Specificity) and (ii)~a structurally matched random feature set, sampled outside concept-matching supernodes with the same feature count and per-layer histogram, fails to produce the same redirection (Matched-Control). Both conditions must hold; failure on either falsifies the label. Definitions~\ref{def:opuseful} and~\ref{def:matched} fix the thresholds we use throughout: a labeled Hit-rate of at least $5$ percentage points, a labeled--random Hit-rate gap of at least $5$ percentage points, and a labeled--random vsMax gap of at least $1.0$ logit.

Across five factual-recall domains on Gemma-2-2B-it with the public CLT-HP dictionary, labeled supernodes satisfy both conditions in four domains (USA, Books, Products, Paintings), with labeled--random vsMax gaps of $+5.17$, $+6.13$, $+3.23$, and $+1.58$ logits at the default operating point of the intervention harness. The Sounds domain fails the matched-control condition (vsMax gap $+0.14$ logits in favor of random) and we report it as a directional negative. The full evaluation covers $94$ seed prompts and $42{,}184$ entity-swap interventions and, to our knowledge, is the largest feature-circuit steering study reported to date; it supports both the primary test above and the diagnostic analyses of Section~\ref{sec:results}. The contemporary feature-circuit study of \citet{hannaameisen2026planning} analyzes five Qwen-3 models on $\leq 100$ examples per model per intervention condition.

We make no claim of full mechanistic identification of the latent variables underlying the supernodes. Our labels are behavioral abstractions with falsifiable causal consequences, at Levels~1--2 of \citet{geiger2025causal}; Level~3 is out of scope. All experiments use Gemma-2-2B-it with CLT-HP on English two-hop prompts, and cross-architecture, cross-dictionary, and multilingual generalization remain open.

The paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item A transparent rule-based pipeline that groups the features of an attribution graph into concept-aligned supernodes from Cross-Prompt Activation Signatures (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A two-condition operational test for supernode labels, with a matched-random-control swap protocol that is grouping-method-agnostic and applicable to any future grouping, including learned ones (Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:expdesign}).
  \item Cross-domain evidence that labeled supernodes pass this test in four of five factual-recall domains, plus a clearly negative fifth domain (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item Diagnostic analyses on the same runs identifying semantic-field choice and multi-feature amplification scale as the primary drivers of intervention quality, and an open release of a $42{,}184$-run intervention dataset, anonymous code, and an interactive demo (Appendix~\ref{sec:repro}).
\end{itemize}
