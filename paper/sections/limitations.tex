\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{1.\ No grouping-method baseline.}
We do not compare against geometric clustering (cosine, layer-adjacency, $k$-means, HDBSCAN). A grouping derived from a behavior-grounded signature outperforms a purely geometric grouping on behavior-grounded metrics nearly by construction, so such a comparison would be structurally biased in our favor; and there is no canonical feature-clustering baseline in the SAE/CLT literature to anchor the comparison. We validate instead via intervention against matched random controls (\cref{def:matched}), which is method-agnostic and applicable to any future grouping, including learned ones, and we encourage comparisons on our harness.

\paragraph{2.\ Attention-circuit blindspot.}
Attribution graphs freeze attention; our swaps run with attention free. The asymmetry plausibly explains part of the 33--79\% miss rate in the strong domains~\cite{ameisen2025circuittracing}. Attention-aware variants are listed as future work (\cref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{3.\ Sounds dataset structural issues.}
Six entities, $12/30$ pairs share answers, $K{=}6$. Sounds fails Condition~2 of \cref{def:opuseful} (\cref{sec:results:negative}) and is excluded from cross-domain inference for the main claim.

\paragraph{4.\ Substring matching and multi-token heuristics.}
The concept-to-supernode matcher uses bidirectional substring matching, which allows reverse containment (e.g., the short supernode name \texttt{is} matching the longer concept \texttt{mississippi}, or \texttt{port} matching \texttt{gulfport}). We audited this behavior and found that entities with the highest reverse-substring rates still perform at or above their dataset mean on vsMax (for example, the USA entity with the highest such rate, Mississippi/Gulfport, has vsMax $3.20$ vs a USA mean of $2.86$), and that within-entity field-additivity does not show a disproportionate drop when reverse-substring matches are added. The labels used in the main-paper experiments were manually inspected against the generated supernode names for every seed; the few cases we found to be unambiguous mismatches were corrected before the sweep. Multi-word answers resolve to their first subword, which can miss or produce spurious matches (e.g., ``St.~Paul'' vs stored ``Saint Paul''). Tightening the matcher is a first-pass improvement for future work.

\paragraph{5.\ Threshold sensitivity is unmeasured.}
Decision-rule thresholds were iteratively refined on pilot circuits; we did not run a formal $\pm 10$--$20\%$ sensitivity sweep. Thresholds are treated as configurable defaults in the released code.

\paragraph{6.\ Cross-prompt robustness is observational.}
The cross-prompt feature-overlap pattern (\S\ref{sec:results:diagnostic:layers}) comes from measuring feature-set overlap across 1{,}607 entity pairs; we did not then \emph{intervene} on shared-early-layer features to verify that ablating them damages many entities uniformly, nor on specialized late-layer features to verify that ablating them damages only one entity. The Jaccard pattern is consistent with a shared early-layer backbone and late-layer specialization, but that reading remains correlational until tested by intervention.

\paragraph{7.\ Within-domain pair-level scaffold prediction is null in USA.}
Scaffold compatibility orders our four strong domains by Hit\% exactly at the between-domain level ($N{=}4$) but does not predict Hit-rate at the pair level within USA, our most statistically powered domain (\cref{sec:results:negative}). The within-domain scaffold range ($[0.38, 0.73]$ for USA) is probably too narrow to discriminate pairs; we therefore limit the scaffold claim to the between-domain level.

\paragraph{8.\ Three levels of interpretive claim; single model and language.}
In the three-level framing of \citet{geiger2025causal}---Level~1 operationally useful labels, Level~2 downstream causal effects validated against controls, Level~3 full mechanistic identification of latent variables---we claim Levels~1 and~2 only; Level~3 is disclaimed. All experiments use Gemma-2-2B-it with CLT-HP and English two-hop prompts (\cref{sec:expdesign:domains}); cross-architecture, cross-dictionary, and multilingual generalization are open.
