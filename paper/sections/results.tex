\paragraph{Specificity decomposition.}
Random controls achieve \emph{equal or higher} suppression than labeled in 3 of 5 domains (Books 75 vs 69\%; Products 87 vs 65\%; Paintings 74 vs 34\%; \cref{appx:specificity}). Suppression is generic; \emph{only targeting is specific}. This invalidates raw Sup\% as a label-quality metric and motivates Hit\%/vsMax/Target-Recovery as the discriminating signals.

\paragraph{Within-disruption-regime evidence (Regime C).}
Classifying every swap by what happens to target and source logits at position 0 yields four regimes (A: clean redirect; C: differential disruption with flip; D: generic disruption; E: pure suppression; \cref{appx:regime}). Random controls concentrate in regime D (42--45\% of pairs); the labeled best variant concentrates in regime A. \emph{Within} regime C, where both logits are disrupted but the target overtakes, labeled drives target above its unsteered baseline 92\% of the time vs.\ 29\% for random in USA (\cref{fig:regime}b). This is the cleanest within-regime signal that ``the model's circuits responded to the target concept'' rather than ``everything got disrupted.''

\paragraph{Statistical reporting.}
Bootstrap 95\% CIs on hit-rate gaps and permutation tests on overlap statistics are reported in \cref{appx:specificity,appx:crossprompt}. With 2{,}450 USA pairs and 240 Books pairs, the observed Hit-rate gaps (67.1 and 63.8 pp) lie far outside any plausible null.

\paragraph{Falsification verdict.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{C1 supported} in USA, Books, Products, Paintings (Specificity gap $\geq 5$ pp \emph{and} vsMax gap $\geq 1.0$ logit; \cref{def:opuseful}).
  \item \textbf{C1 not supported in Sounds} (vsMax gap $+0.14$, Hit-rate gap $+16.7$ pp on tiny $N{=}30$). Reported as a directional negative result in \cref{sec:results:negative}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{C2: The harness surfaces four mechanistic regularities}
\label{sec:results:c2}

\subsubsection{C2.1: Less is more---input-field features interfere with steering}
\label{sec:results:c2:lessismore}
In 4 of 5 domains, the intermediate$+$answer subset beats the all-3-field intervention by 9--33~pp (\cref{tab:fieldadd}):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item USA state$+$capital $38.8\%$ vs.\ all-3 $24.7\%$ ($+14.1$ pp);
  \item Books book$+$author $37.1\%$ vs.\ all-3 $3.8\%$ ($+33.3$ pp);
  \item Products company$+$founder $24.2\%$ vs.\ all-3 $15.2\%$ ($+9.0$ pp);
  \item Sounds sound$+$animal $20.0\%$ vs.\ all-3 $0.0\%$ ($+20.0$ pp);
  \item Paintings: weak baseline, weak gain ($+3.4$ pp).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Interpretation.} Input-field features encode what the model \emph{reads}, not what it should \emph{produce}. Including them activates competing circuits that dilute or interfere with the answer signal. Cross-domain single-field aggregates confirm the role gradient: intermediate (Hit $14.6\%$) $>$ answer ($8.8\%$) $>$ input ($6.1\%$) (\cref{appx:fieldadd}).

\subsubsection{C2.2: Default $M{=}20$ is over-steering; the winning $M$ distribution is bimodal}
\label{sec:results:c2:msearch}
Adaptive $M$-search rescues 452 missed pairs across all conditions. The winning-$M$ distribution is bimodal at $M{\sim}2.4$ (47\% of labeled hits) and $M{\sim}6.9$ (40\%) (\cref{fig:mhist}). Two control experiments confirm the mechanism rather than the tuning:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{High-$M$ rescue is null.} Testing $M{\in}\{50, 100, 200\}$ on near-miss pairs (80 runs across 3 domains) produced \emph{0 new hits}. KL saturates above $M{\sim}50$ while output quality continues to worsen.
  \item \textbf{Top-$k$ rescue is null.} Testing top-$k$ features by graph influence ($k{\in}\{1, 3, 5, 10\}$) at $M{\in}[20, 200]$ on Products produced \emph{0/96 hits}. Only the full feature set ($k{=}67$) achieves hits.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Interpretation.} The steering signal is \emph{distributed}, not concentrated. Multi-feature amplification at high $M$ produces distributed noise that overwhelms the target signal. This contrasts structurally with the single-feature paradigm in which high $M$ is a targeted directional push~\cite{templeton2024scaling}.

\subsubsection{C2.3: Backbone-and-specialization layer gradient}
\label{sec:results:c2:layers}
Cross-prompt scalable analysis on 1{,}607 intra-domain pairs across 5 domains (103 entities; \cref{appx:crossprompt}): activation stability $>90\%$ in all domains; Jaccard overlap $0.29$--$0.62$ (significantly above chance, $p<0.001$ via permutation at multiple pool sizes). Early/late Jaccard ratio is $1.4$--$3.0\times$ in 4 of 5 domains: early layers shared backbone, late layers entity-specific.

\subsubsection{C2.4: Scaffold influence is a perfect ordinal predictor of domain-level success}
\label{sec:results:c2:scaffold}
Graph scaffold compatibility---the fraction of total graph influence carried by features with \emph{identical} supernode assignment across two entity graphs---perfectly orders the four strong domains by Hit\% at the all-3-field labeled baseline (\cref{fig:scaffold}a; Spearman $\rho{=}1.0$, $N{=}4$):

The late-layer ($\geq L_{16}$) scaffold gradient is even steeper (USA 16.3\% vs Books 4.3\%; \cref{fig:scaffold}b). \emph{Within-domain pair-level prediction is null in USA} (Q1 27.8\% vs Q4 24.3\%, no monotone trend; the within-USA scaffold range $[0.38, 0.73]$ is too narrow to discriminate pairs) and only weak in smaller domains (Products residualized $r{=}+0.29$, $N{=}132$). We report this honestly in \cref{sec:results:negative}.

\paragraph{Falsification verdict on C2.}
Three of four sub-claims (C2.1, C2.2, C2.3) are supported with directional thresholds met in 4--5 domains. The cross-domain ordinal prediction in C2.4 is supported ($\rho{=}1.0$, $N{=}4$); the \emph{within-domain pair-level} prediction in C2.4 is null in USA and weak elsewhere, reported as a sub-claim negative.

\subsection{Where the method fails}
\label{sec:results:negative}

We commit two named negative results to the main body, in line with the workshop's call for rigorous negative findings.

\paragraph{Negative result 1: Sounds.}
On 6 entities, 12/30 non-identity pairs share the answer ``brown''. Random hits (3.3\%) come predominantly from these shared-answer pairs; matched-control vsMax exceeds labeled vsMax by $0.14$ logits. \emph{Sounds fails Condition~2 of \cref{def:opuseful}.} We report this as a domain-level negative and exclude Sounds from cross-domain inference for C1; we also identify three structural causes (small $N$, shared-answer artefacts, $K{=}6$ thin logit margins; \cref{appx:negative}).

\paragraph{Negative result 2: Within-USA scaffold prediction.}
The scaffold metric is a perfect cross-domain ordinal predictor of Hit\% but a null within-domain pair-level predictor in USA, the most powered dataset (Q1--Q4 hit rates 27.8/24.7/24.3/24.3\%; $|r|<0.10$ for all variants; $N{=}2{,}450$). The within-USA scaffold range is too narrow to discriminate pairs. \emph{The scaffold story is a between-domain story; we explicitly do not claim within-domain predictive utility.}
