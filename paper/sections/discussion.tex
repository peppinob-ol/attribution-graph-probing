\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Methodological lessons.}
Three findings generalize beyond probe prompting.
\emph{(i) Match random controls structurally.} Without matching feature count, per-layer histogram, and concept-exclusion, the labeled--random gap is misstated; random controls produce \emph{more} suppression than labeled in 3/5 domains. \emph{(ii) Default $M{=}20$ does not transfer.} Over-steering in the multi-feature CLT setting; winning $M$ is bimodal at $\sim\!2.4$/$\sim\!6.9$. Recommend a coarse $M$ sweep as standard practice. \emph{(iii) Decompose by semantic field role.} The less-is-more effect (14--33~pp gain from removing input-field features) shows that input features encode what the model \emph{reads}, not what it should \emph{produce}.

\paragraph{What the labels mean.}
Supernode labels are behavioral abstractions; they pass an operational test against matched controls but do not constitute mechanistic identifications of latent variables (Level~3 of \citealp{geiger2025causal}).

\paragraph{Future work.}
(a) Attention-aware variants; (b) cross-architecture replication on Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3; (c) multilingual extension; (d) within-domain pair-level scaffold predictor. All artifacts will be de-anonymized at camera ready.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Probe prompting demonstrates that circuit-level interpretability of attribution graphs can be partially automated using a transparent rule-based pipeline validated by intervention against matched random controls. The two-condition definition of an operationally useful label gives the harness a sharp falsification criterion; its substantive findings (less-is-more, over-steering, scaffold) inform best practice in the steering literature.
