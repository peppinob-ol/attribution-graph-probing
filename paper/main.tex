\begin{abstract}
Manual interpretation of attribution graphs is slow (approximately 2 hours per prompt) and yields inconsistent taxonomies. We introduce \emph{probe prompting}, a transparent rule-based pipeline that converts a graph into concept-aligned supernodes via Cross-Prompt Activation Signatures (CPAS). We define an \emph{operationally useful} label as one that satisfies two conditions: (i) \textbf{Specificity}---intervention via the labeled supernode redirects output toward the specific concept, and (ii) \textbf{Matched-Control}---intervention via a structurally matched random feature set (same count, same per-layer histogram, sampled outside concept-matching supernodes) does not produce the same effect. Across 5 domains and 42{,}184 entity-swap interventions, labeled supernodes satisfy both conditions in 4 of 5 domains (USA 67.3 vs 0.2\% hit; Books 63.8 vs 0.0\%), with Sounds (6 entities, structural confounds) failing Condition~2 (matched-control vsMax gap $+0.14$) and reported as a directional negative. The harness surfaces three regularities: input-field features \emph{interfere} with steering (less-is-more, +14--33 pp), the canonical $M{=}20$ default is \emph{over-steering} (winning $M$ is bimodal at $\sim$2.4 and $\sim$6.9), and graph scaffold compatibility is a perfect ordinal predictor of domain-level success. Code, datasets, and an interactive demo are released anonymously.
\end{abstract}
